## 2. §5.7 Platform Economies — replaced in full

> Any exchange economy operated on Corporation platforms shall conform to Janus Facing Architecture. Where an obligation below carries a registry ID, these bylaws bind it as an instrument-level obligation.
>
> Money is created at the moment of exchange — one balance down, one up, always summing to zero (L1).
>
> Settlement begins as pre-funded escrow: collateralized, no negative balances, no trust extended (L10).
>
> A deployment may operate a hybrid stage in which escrow and mutual credit run across the same system and each prosumer decides which they accept.
>
> The switch from escrow to a hybrid system or to full mutual credit occurs only after the operator builds capacity, the prosumer network is notified, and a completed regulatory review is published to the governance layer (L10). Publication of the regulatory review to the governance layer is the application; no further Board approval attaches. The Secretariat records the published review in the register of §7.3.
>
> Credit units are earned for value provided to the network; they are never sold, never purchasable, and never redeemable for fiat currency (L2); denomination against fiat is a unit of account, never a backing.
>
> Issuance is gated by the covenant; how much any member may draw is a community-wide limit set by the platform operator — one number for everyone, never per-member, and never derived from reputation (L9).
>
> Each platform's unit remains sovereign and non-convertible to any other platform's unit (L3); value stays home, and only truth crosses (L4).
>
> The operator manages the economy it hosts: it annotates defaulted balances by kind — deceased, departed, or adjudicated — never erasing them (L6), and publishes its economy's trailing default rate.
>
> The Treasurer reports the status of any economy under this Section, including each platform's published trailing default rate, in the quarterly public reports under §3.7.

## 3. §2.7 Operator Membership — Recognition and Evidence paragraphs replaced

**Recognition — bootstrap and maturity**, replaced with:

> Until the record layer of the architecture is operating — the public chain distributed across substrate, with each platform maintaining at least two independent witnesses — operator members are recognized by Board action with notice to the membership, beginning from the genesis operator set of §12.3(c). Once the record layer is operating, recognition shall be read against the public chain: an operator is recognized by the recorded history of its operation. A platform with fewer than two independent witnesses must label itself unfederated, and its operator does not qualify for recognition while so labeled. Operators under common control are not independent of one another; the transition of recognition modes is governed by §12.3(b).

**Evidence discipline**, first sentence replaced with:

> A finding under this Section rests on conduct read as structure from the public chain and the six-holder record — each transactor's record, the operator's, both witnesses', and the chain itself — including dwell distributions, dismissal patterns, and collusion signatures — and on adjudicated harm claims from genuine counterparties.

(Remainder of the paragraph unchanged, except "Until the witnessed record layer is operating" → "Until the record layer is operating.")

**Conduct standard**, "Harmful environments" clause: "users of the operator's platform" → "prosumers of the operator's platform."

## 4. §2.8 Federation Membership — new final paragraph before Non-derogation

> **Federation requirement.** A platform must maintain at least two independent witnesses to remain federated. A platform that cannot must label itself unfederated, and its operator's federation membership is suspended until the requirement is restored. Witnessing itself is a function of the substrate layer and is not otherwise a concern of these bylaws.

## 5. New §2.9: Appeals and Readmission

> The governance venue for appeals under the architecture's dispute design is the office of the Vice President. A prosumer whose trust has been suspended or whose expulsion has been referred to governance, and an operator disqualified under §2.7, may appeal by submitting their case to the office of the Vice President. The submission shall contain no personally identifying information; Article XI §11.2 governs.
>
> Upon receipt, the Vice President's office shall post the case to the appropriate Federation Channel for a rating period of one (1) week under the LBTAS rating procedure. Each member of the Vice President's circle (#desk-vice-president) may cast exactly one rating on the offense during the appeal period. No member of the membership is required to vote. If the mode of the ratings recorded is negative one (−1), the appellant is not readmitted. If no ratings are recorded during the period, the Vice President, as the office's elected delegate, shall decide the appeal. A denied appeal may be resubmitted after a cooldown period of one (1) week.
>
> Readmission restores the trust gate, franchise, or federation membership that the underlying disposition removed, on any terms the disposing bodies set under §2.7. Every appeal, rating period, and disposition is recorded in the register of §7.3 as annotations, never erasures.

## 6. §12.3(b) Franchise maturation — replaced

> (b) Franchise maturation and sunset. Operator membership is adopted now at §2.7; what remains committed is its maturation. When the record layer of the architecture is operating — the public chain live on substrate, platforms maintaining at least two independent witnesses, and dispute filings and resolutions committed to the chain — such that operator conduct can be read from recorded structure rather than from self-attestation, operator recognition shall shift from Board recognition to recognition against the public chain per §2.7, and the Board shall bring to the membership a proposed amendment sunsetting the subscription qualifier of §12.2(a), leaving operator membership as the sole qualification for the franchise. Any such amendment shall preserve one person, one vote; operator status shall act as a qualifier on that single vote and never as a multiplier. The amendment is adopted, or declined, by the membership under Article IX, on the membership's own timing.

## 7. §12.1 — added at the end of the Section

> The architecture document ships with an executable conformance suite that maintains a registry of invariants cited by stable IDs. Where these bylaws carry an architecture obligation, they cite the registry ID that binds it (L1, L2, L3, L4, L6, L9, L10 at §5.7; the append-only register of §7.3 carries the discipline of L6). The suite designates certain invariants as enforceable only by a governance instrument; this instrument is where they bind. The Secretariat shall run the conformance suite against the architecture document as part of register maintenance under §7.3.

## 9. Amendment Notes addition (for the v7.1 notes block)

> The recall provisions of §3.8 and §4.5 and the Maximum Observational Diversity challenge of §3.3 and §2.8 are, at instrument level, the carriers of two concepts the architecture document deliberately leaves to bylaws: recallable delegation and the reopening of decided matters. The 2026-08-27 architecture revision moved operator economic management (beyond the credit limit), assembly and recall mechanics, and reopening mechanics to the bylaws tier; this amendment absorbs them at §5.7, §3.8/§4.5, and §3.3/§2.8 respectively, and establishes the appeal venue of §2.9.
